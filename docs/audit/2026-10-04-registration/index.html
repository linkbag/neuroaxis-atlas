What the evidence shows

R1 · Confirmed CT orientation error

Front and back are reversed in archived CT

DICOM's positive patient y direction is posterior. The CT baker maps it to positive atlas z, which the app labels anterior. Independent reconstruction matches the archived CT pixels exactly.

Correct conversion: (left, superior, anterior) = (xLPS, zLPS, −yLPS). Correcting the sign still requires a new translation and registration.

R2 · Confirmed CT placement error

The named brainstem level samples cerebrum

The atlas pontomedullary level y=−24 currently selects original CT S=−70.8 mm. That source plane contains cerebral hemispheres and lateral ventricles. The CT overlay therefore cannot validate the simulated brainstem.

Four upper teaching levels contain no source CT data under the old translation. This does not establish missing upper-head acquisition coverage.

R3 · Measured MRI coverage mismatch

23–24% of cortical vertices exceed the MRI grid

The displayed MRI ends at y=+85 au; the current cortical model reaches +113.76. Left/right cortical vertex fractions outside the baked field are 23.28% / 23.64%.

These are vertex counts, not a percentage of brain volume. The full original MRI adds image coverage; it does not automatically solve boundary alignment.

R4 · Visible MRI/model boundary mismatch

The brainstem fit does not establish a global fit

Several central brainstem sections show compatible MRI silhouettes. Cortical, callosal and cerebellar contours depart from the visible tissue boundaries in other sections, including the full source MRI.

The current MRI affine and model geometry stay unchanged for review. Fine nuclei, pathways and nerves cannot all be certified from these scans.

R5 · Acceptance evidence needs improvement

Threshold overlap measures head intensity

The old fitter compares a model brain mask with thresholded head intensities. Its CT cutoff of uint8≥8 represents roughly −16.24 HU, rather than the 14 HU written in its comment. Local intensity gradients can select the wrong anatomical edge.

A higher mask overlap score would not establish correct pons, cortex or ventricular correspondence.

R6 / R7 · Smaller tradeoffs

Minor numerical differences can be tolerated

Using mean CT slice spacing instead of actual positions introduces up to 1.458 mm of source-position error. Manifest spacing rounding contributes only 0.00257 au; MRI reconstruction differs by at most one gray level.

Use physical CT slice positions in future work. No correction is needed for negligible rounding or quantization.

Plane and contour mechanics pass

Simulated sections are consistent with the model

The app's pipeline passes for all 138 parts and 599,204 triangles, producing 386 loops across 13 test planes. Shared plane checks pass 10,827 assertions. This establishes coordinate and cut consistency; anatomical fidelity still depends on the underlying model and registration.

The public app offers MRI and Simulated only. CT/photos remain excluded, and the 3D inset is simulated only. The live MRI alignment notice already describes the affine as approximate.

Independent checks and their limits

  1. Decode all source CT DICOMs using pydicom, sort by physical position and inspect orientation, spacing, rescale parameters and gaps. The stack is 512×512×463 with 0.489062 mm in-plane spacing; actual slice steps are 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 mm.
  2. Inspect the original 176×280×288 NIfTI T1 with nibabel, including sform/qform and patient-axis conversion. MRI and CT are different subjects and sources, so correspondence must use homologous anatomy.
  3. Independently reconstruct source-to-atlas sampling. All 126,198 CT sample stations agree exactly with the committed archive. MRI agrees exactly at 98.68%, and within one gray level at every station.
  4. Load all 138 GLBs with node transforms, measure field coverage and derive contours using independent triangle intersections. Inspect all 17 transverse levels plus three sagittal and three coronal planes.
  5. Cross-check anatomical interpretation with Haines 8th ed., pp. 130–131 and 176–177, and Blumenfeld 2nd ed., p. 494. Textbook images remain in private scratch storage and are not reproduced here.

This is an engineering and anatomical review with documented limits. It is not independent neuroradiologist certification, subject-specific segmentation or population morphometry. No new validated affine is claimed.

Recommended next work after your review

  1. Correct archived CT source geometry and establish a new registration. Repair the AP sign and use actual slice positions. Refit and inspect landmarks across all three planes. CT stays excluded while this is reviewed.
  2. Prepare an MRI field extension as a candidate. Preserve the current affine and existing lattice initially, then compare old stations and inspect the added image region. Expanding the grid does not by itself correct model boundaries.
  3. Review whole-brain model geometry and image alignment together. Include brainstem junctions, fourth ventricle, callosal genu/splenium, lateral ventricular horns, cortical poles/vertex and bilateral boundaries. Hold some landmarks out of the fit.
  4. Document accepted teaching tradeoffs per region. Small interpolation and quantization differences are reasonable. An AP reflection, wrong head level or large missing display region needs explicit correction or explanation.
  5. Submit the candidate app for your review before deployment. No new registration, anatomy, UI settings or asset bake is applied by this audit.
