# Tract scientific audit

All 23 authored tract records reviewed. Content is a sourced teaching summary; spatial accuracy is not certified.

## Corrections

### Corticospinal tract (lateral) (`tract-corticospinal-lateral`)

Changed: origin, target, decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** Most fibres (approximately 85-90%, with individual variation) cross at the caudal medullary pyramidal decussation to form the lateral tract. Anterior corticospinal fibres are a separate, mainly axial pathway with bilateral/segmental projections.
- **somatotopy:** Cortical and supraspinal motor organization is topographic. A fixed cervical-medial/sacral-lateral lamination of the human lateral spinal tract should not be used as a reliable clinical rule; contemporary evidence challenges the classical schematic map.
- **function:** Major descending pathway for voluntary limb movement, especially fractionated distal movements. Injury may produce weakness and upper motor neuron signs; acute lesions can initially cause reduced tone and reflexes.

Sources: Blumenfeld, Neuroanatomy through Clinical Cases, 2nd ed. (2010), Ch. 6: Corticospinal Tract and Other Motor Pathways, pp. 231-242 (PDF pp. 257-268); Lemon & Morecraft (2022), A critical reappraisal of corticospinal tract somatotopy, Brain, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34767532/ (evidence review; classical spinal lamination disputed)

### Corticobulbar tract (`tract-corticobulbar`)

Changed: target, decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** Projections cross at several brainstem levels and are predominantly bilateral. Lower facial motor neurons and genioglossus receive important contralateral input; this is not a single compact decussation.
- **somatotopy:** Orofacial representation lies mainly in inferolateral motor cortex; descending fibres pass through the genu/adjacent internal capsule. Brainstem projections are distributed rather than a precisely mapped single tube.
- **function:** Contributes to voluntary orofacial, swallowing and speech movements through cranial motor networks. Bilateral innervation gives partial redundancy, with clinically important exceptions.

### Corticopontine tract (`tract-corticopontine`)

Changed: target, decussation, function, clinical, waypoints, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** Corticopontine fibres themselves principally terminate ipsilaterally. Crossing belongs to the downstream pontocerebellar projection, not to this tract.
- **function:** Relays widespread cortical information to pontine nuclei, forming the input limb of corticopontocerebellar circuits involved in coordination and motor learning.

### Medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF) (`tract-mlf`)

Changed: synonyms, origin, target, decussation, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** Abducens internuclear axons cross in the pons soon after leaving the abducens nucleus, then ascend in the opposite MLF to medial-rectus neurons of CN III. Other MLF components include crossed and uncrossed vestibular projections.

### Central tegmental tract (`tract-central-tegmental`)

Changed: origin, target, decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** The rubro-olivary component is principally uncrossed. Dentatorubral input crosses via the superior cerebellar peduncle; olivocerebellar output crosses to the opposite cerebellar cortex via the inferior peduncle.
- **somatotopy:** No fine somatotopic representation is provided.
- **function:** The rubro-olivary component forms part of the dentato-rubro-olivary circuit. The return route involves olivary climbing fibres to cerebellar cortex and deep-nuclear circuitry, not a simple direct olive-to-dentate synapse.

### Dorsal column-medial lemniscus pathway (`tract-dcml`)

Changed: origin, target, decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** Second-order axons cross in the caudal medulla as internal arcuate fibres (sensory decussation).
- **somatotopy:** Dorsal columns: lower body medial (gracile), upper body lateral (cuneate). Medial lemniscus: feet more ventral in medulla; arms more medial and legs more lateral in pons/midbrain as the ribbon rotates. These are broad teaching relationships.

### Spinothalamic tract (anterolateral system) (`tract-spinothalamic`)

Changed: origin, target, decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** Most second-order axons cross through the spinal anterior white commissure, often within one or a few segments of entry. Ascending brainstem fibres already represent the opposite body side.

### Anterior spinocerebellar tract (`tract-anterior-spinocerebellar`)

Changed: origin, target, decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, meshes, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** First crosses in the spinal ventral/anterior white commissure. Many fibres recross on entering/within the cerebellum after traversing the superior cerebellar peduncle; this is not the caudal-midbrain decussation of cerebellar output fibres.
- **somatotopy:** Ascends near the peripheral anterolateral cord/brainstem before joining the superior peduncle; exact fibre ordering is not represented.
- **function:** Reports integrated spinal motor/interneuronal activity to the cerebellum, complementing dorsal spinocerebellar sensory feedback.

### Optic radiation (`tract-optic-radiation`)

Changed: decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** No additional crossing in LGN or optic radiation. Nasal retinal fibres crossed at the optic chiasm; each radiation carries the contralateral visual hemifield.
- **somatotopy:** Temporal Meyer loop carries superior-field information to the inferior bank of calcarine cortex; parietal fibres carry inferior-field information to the superior bank. Central vision has a large posterior cortical representation. Neither this schematic tube nor field congruity establishes a precise individual fibre map.
- **function:** Geniculocalcarine fibres link LGN to striate cortex through retrolenticular internal capsule and temporal/parietal white matter. The displayed curve summarizes a fan-shaped system with distinct branches.

Sources: Blumenfeld, Neuroanatomy through Clinical Cases, 2nd ed. (2010), Ch. 11: Visual System, pp. 463-476 (PDF pp. 489-502); Yogarajah et al. (2009), Brain 132:1656-1668, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19460796/ (individual Meyer-loop variability)

### Cingulum (`tract-cingulum`)

Changed: origin, target, decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** No obligatory midline crossing: principally an intrahemispheric association bundle. Interhemispheric communication uses additional commissural pathways.
- **somatotopy:** Topographic association bundle curving around the corpus callosum and continuing toward parahippocampal white matter; not all axons traverse its entire length.
- **function:** Connects medial association and limbic cortices, contributing to attention, memory and affective networks. Its parahippocampal connections form part of the classical Papez teaching circuit.

### Uncinate fasciculus (`tract-uncinate-fasciculus`)

Changed: decussation, somatotopy, function, clinical, refs, geometryNote.

- **decussation:** Principally intrahemispheric; curves around the limen insulae between anterior temporal and orbitofrontal regions.
- **somatotopy:** Hook-shaped association bundle; no simple body map. Group-level asymmetry depends on the measurement method and should not be taught as invariably larger on the left.
